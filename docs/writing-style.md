# Eric Reyes: writing guide

Use this guide for journal entries, About copy, page descriptions, and supporting explanations. Keep navigation and controls clear.

## Basis and limits

The user supplied handwritten technical study notes as writing samples and asked for the site to follow that style. The samples use topic headings, short definitions, direct explanations, numbered steps, lists, and occasional brief examples. They provide stronger evidence about explanatory structure than personal storytelling. This guide is an editorial adaptation, not a claim that every sample sentence was originally composed by Eric.

## Voice

- State the topic or purpose early.
- Use plain words and direct verbs: identify, check, build, test, document.
- Keep sentences focused on one main point. Use short paragraphs and natural contractions. Sound like a student explaining the work, not a textbook.
- Organize technical explanations by topic, task, or step.
- Explain what something does before adding detail.
- Use numbered lists for a sequence and bullets for parallel items.
- Use an example when it makes the explanation clearer. Label hypothetical examples with “if” or “for example”; never present them as completed work.
- For personal entries, use first person and confirmed facts. State goals as goals.
- Keep spelling and grammar clean. Do not imitate shorthand errors or turn every paragraph into fragments.
- Avoid promotional slogans, abstract metaphors, inflated claims, and repetitive summaries.

## Post structure

For an introduction: A little about me, What I want to improve, How I plan to work, What I’ll write here.

For a development entry: Problem or feature, Approach or steps, Test and result, Changes or next step. Adapt the headings to the actual work; do not force a template onto every post.

## Example

Instead of: “A growing collection of decisions, challenges, and lessons from building software.”

Use: “Here I record what I’m working on, how I approach it, and what I learn.”
